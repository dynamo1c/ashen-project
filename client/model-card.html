Model Card & Limitations

What this system is, what it is not, and where it is weakest.

← BACK TO DASHBOARD
Decision-support, not decision-making. Every score, flag, and match surfaced by Ashen Protocol — anomaly severity, recidivism risk level, MO similarity percentage, syndicate linkage — is an investigative prioritization signal for a human analyst to review, not a verdict. Nothing in this system determines guilt, authorizes an arrest, or should be cited as sole grounds for a stop, search, or charge. All outputs require human corroboration against case evidence before any operational action.

1. WHAT EACH MODEL ACTUALLY DOES

2. KNOWN LIMITATIONS OF THE CURRENT BUILD

  • Anomaly baselines are a fixed demonstration dataset. The five anomalies shown are precomputed illustrative examples (see anomaly_engine.js), not a live rolling z-score recomputed against the datastore on every request. A production deployment needs a real multi-month rolling baseline per district/crime-type before this becomes a live detector.
  • Recidivism risk scores are frequently synthetic. base_risk_score for any offender without a live datastore record is generated as Math.random() * 85 + 10 at FIR-inject time or at demo-seed time — a placeholder, not an actuarial or ML-derived score. When the live offender count is below the 1,480-profile demo target, the remaining profiles shown in the Recidivism Matrix are entirely generated (see the real_profile_count field returned by /api/analytics/recidivism and the SAMPLE DATA badge on that panel).
  • MO similarity is lexical, not semantic. Narratives describing the same MO in different words will under-match; narratives describing different MOs with incidentally overlapping phrasing will over-match. Treat a high match percentage as "worth a human read," not as confirmation.
  • Cold-start instability. Districts or crime categories with few historical incidents produce unstable or undefined z-scores and thin comparison pools for MO matching — both models are weakest exactly where crime is rarest, which is the opposite of where over-confidence would be safe.
  • Offline/local demo data is synthetic. Whenever the Zoho Catalyst datastore is unreachable (e.g. running via node index.js outside Catalyst, or a fresh unseeded environment), every analytics route falls back to generated sample data shaped like the real schema. The SAMPLE DATA badge introduced on every panel makes this state visible at a glance — if it is not showing, the panel is reading live data.
  • LLM briefing is not independently verified. The Cerebras-generated strategic briefing can occasionally paraphrase or round figures inconsistently with the source panels. It is a synthesis aid, not a certified report.

3. BIAS & FALSE-POSITIVE BEHAVIOR

The Recidivism Matrix groups offenders by age and gender bands (e.g. "Youth Male 18–25"). Demographic cohort statistics like these can encode or amplify existing bias in historical enforcement patterns if used to direct patrols, stops, or resource allocation at named individuals or communities. This tool reports aggregate cohort rates for strategic planning context only — it must never be used to justify individualized suspicion of a person based on their age or gender group membership.

False positives to expect in normal operation:

  • An anomaly "CRITICAL surge" can reflect a genuine one-time event (a single large case, a reporting-drive, or a data-entry batch) rather than an emerging pattern.
  • A "100% MO match" reflects near-identical wording within the same crime category, not proof of a shared perpetrator — always open both source FIRs before acting.
  • A CRITICAL/HIGH recidivism-risk label on a synthetic (non-live) profile carries no real-world meaning — check the SAMPLE DATA badge first.

4. INTENDED USE

Ashen Protocol is built as an investigative triage and situational-awareness dashboard for KSP analysts: to surface where to look next, not to conclude what happened. Every number on screen should be treated as a lead to verify against primary case records, not as a finding to cite on its own.
