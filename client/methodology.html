How the anomaly, recidivism, and MO-similarity scores shown in the dashboard are computed.

1. CRIME ANOMALY DETECTION — Z-SCORE MODEL

The Crime Anomaly Radar flags a district/crime-type pair as anomalous when its current monthly incident count deviates sharply from its own historical baseline. The model is a standard z-score (standard-score) test:

z = (x − μ) / σ
x — current month's incident count for a given (district, crime_head) pair
μ — rolling historical monthly average for that same pair (baseline_monthly_avg)
σ — standard deviation of that pair's monthly counts over the baseline window

A pair is surfaced as an anomaly when |z| > 2.0 (roughly the top/bottom ~2.3% of a normal distribution — a conventional two-sigma significance threshold):

ConditionClassificationInterpretation
z ≥ +2.0ABNORMAL_SPIKESudden surge — possible syndicate outbreak, new MO, or reporting-drive artifact
z ≤ −2.0UNDERREPORTING_DROPSudden drop — possible FIR suppression, station-level under-registration
|z| < 2.0Not flaggedWithin normal month-to-month variance

Percentage change is reported alongside the z-score for interpretability: percentage_change = (x − μ) / μ × 100.

Current build status: the anomaly engine (anomaly_engine.js) ships with five illustrative district/crime-type baselines precomputed from the seed dataset, rather than a live rolling recomputation against the datastore on every request. The z-score formula above is what the model is designed around and what those five baselines were computed with — see the Model Card for the full disclosure on this limitation.

2. RECIDIVISM COHORT DEFINITION

The Demographic & Recidivism Propensity Matrix groups the offender population into four non-exclusive demographic cohorts and reports two statistics per cohort:

CohortDefinition
Youth High-Riskgender = MALE and 18 ≤ age ≤ 25
Adult Serialgender = MALE and 26 ≤ age ≤ 35
Female Cohortgender = FEMALE
Senior Cohortage ≥ 45
reoffending_rate(cohort) = count(members with prior_offenses ≥ 2) / count(cohort members) × 100
avg_priors(cohort) = Σ prior_offenses / count(cohort members)

A profile is counted as "recidivism-indicative" once it carries two or more prior offenses on record (prior_offenses ≥ 2) — this is a fixed threshold, not a learned classifier boundary. recidivism_risk_level per profile (CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM) is derived from base_risk_score via the same three-tier threshold used platform-wide: ≥70 HIGH-band, ≥35 MED-band, else LOW-band (see toRiskScored() in index.js), with the top band relabeled CRITICAL specifically in the recidivism view for >75.

3. MODUS OPERANDI SIMILARITY SCORING — TF-IDF WEIGHTED COSINE SIMILARITY

The "Automated Behavioral MO Similarity" panel on each case dossier finds other FIRs whose free-text incident narrative reads similarly to the case being viewed. This is a lexical (term-frequency based) similarity engine, hand-implemented in index.js — not a neural/semantic embedding model. It runs in three steps:

Step 1 — Tokenize

Each MO narrative is lowercased, stripped of punctuation, split on whitespace, and filtered against a ~90-word English stop-word list, keeping only tokens longer than 2 characters.

Step 2 — Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency

TF(t, d) = count(t in d) / |d|
IDF(t) = ln(N / df(t)) + 1.0
N — number of documents in the comparison pool (the target case + up to 100 same-category candidates)
df(t) — number of documents in that pool containing term t

Step 3 — Cosine Similarity

similarity(A, B) = (Σ TFIDF_A(t) · TFIDF_B(t)) / (‖TFIDF_A‖ · ‖TFIDF_B‖)
where TFIDF(t) = TF(t, d) × IDF(t), and ‖v‖ is the Euclidean norm of vector v

Candidates are ranked by cosine similarity, filtered to similarity_score > 0.05, and the top 5 are returned with their matched terms and a rounded similarity_percent. The comparison pool is scoped to FIRs sharing the same crime_head category as the target case, so a "100% match" reflects near-identical wording within that category — not a claim of certainty about shared authorship or a common perpetrator.

Why "TF-IDF", not "semantic": this engine matches on shared vocabulary, not meaning — two narratives describing the same MO in different words will score lower than two narratives describing different MOs with overlapping phrasing. A semantic/embedding-based upgrade is a natural next iteration; the current engine is intentionally simple, dependency-free, and fully auditable — every score can be traced back to the exact matched terms shown in the UI.
